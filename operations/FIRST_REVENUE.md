# First revenue experiment — proposal, not launched

## Outcome

One buyer pays for and accepts a narrowly specified deliverable made with existing tools. No promise of revenue tonight. A repository or demo alone is not validation.

## Candidate order (hypotheses, not market findings)

1. Client-provided spreadsheet cleanup plus a concise decision report: test if a reachable buyer has a messy file and a deadline. Use deterministic processing and reviewed documents.
2. Client-owned content repackaging: test a fixed set of resize/export deliverables with Adobe; generation only if required and budgeted.
3. Repair one existing internal workflow: inspect a client's authorized automation, agree acceptance criteria, deliver a tested repair and handoff. Avoid promising a hosted multi-client platform.

Choose the first candidate with an identifiable reachable buyer, authorized input and an acceptance test. No current buyer, demand, sales channel or market price has been verified.

## Bounded session

- 20 minutes: choose one buyer category, one output, required input and deadline.
- 40 minutes: create a synthetic/owned-data sample with existing tools.
- 20 minutes: inspect quality and calculate labor-inclusive margin.
- 20 minutes: prepare a short offer and evidence; owner approves recipient and outreach.
- After interest: agree scope, fee, payment method, revision limit and refund terms; owner handles financial commitments.

If no reachable buyer or authorized sample exists, stop building and record that dependency. If the sample cannot meet acceptance within the time/cost cap, narrow scope once, then stop. Do not change the success criterion to views, likes or number of integrations.

## Evidence and adaptation

Track contacted prospects only after authorized outreach: qualified conversations, offers, payments collected, refunds, delivery hours and acceptance.
After five qualified conversations with no willingness to pay, review the offer and rejection reasons before more building. Five is a proposed experiment threshold, not statistical proof.
After a paid pilot, automate only the repeated bottleneck; maintain a manual fallback.
